> ### What This Note Claims — and What It Does Not
> - **What it is:** a statistical screening tool, that is, a set of hypothesis tests that can rule out one model (a passive mixture of independent decays) from noisy decay or duration data. It is tested on simulations and public datasets and is not yet validated on `CFTR` recordings.
> - **Claimed:** two closed-form estimators, `W_split` and `W_diff,split`, remove noise-squaring bias and an unknown constant background from a three-point curvature test of decay curves. A significant downward bend rules out a passive mixture of independent decays; it does not identify what replaces it.
> - **Measured:** the estimators were run on `7` public experimental datasets (optical, X-ray, patch-clamp, molecular-motor, and mucus-gel). Three control analyses on the cached data (`benchmark/controls/`) and a false-alarm calibration by simulation (`benchmark/nullcal/`) are included; four results are reported as weak or unsupported.
> - **For event lists, use every event:** when the data are burst or dwell durations, a whole-sample variability test checks the same null hypothesis as the three-point test with far more power. In simulation with two sequential steps of similar length (close to the most favourable two-step case) it detects the two-step structure `91.2%` of the time with `100` bursts, where the three-bin test reaches `29.5%` at `300`. When one step is five times faster than the other this falls to `32%` (`76%` with `300` bursts) (`benchmark/whole_sample/`). The statistic is classical; this package contributes a worked, calibrated procedure (dead time, frame quantization, per-patch combination), and none of those steps is new on its own.
> - **Simulated only:** every `CFTR` result. No `CFTR` recording or patient sample is analyzed.
> - **Not claimed:** clinical relevance; that this screen beats likelihood fitting (in simulation under a correctly specified two-step model it does not: `~100%` vs. `29.5%` power at `N = 300`, `α = 0.01`; the whole-sample test has not been compared with likelihood fitting below `N = 300`, where both are near `100%`).
> - **Reading the tables:** `z` is an estimate divided by its standard error (plug-in, exact, or jackknife over `K = 12–16` blocks, sectors, or `q`-vectors; sectors and `q`-vectors of one sample are not independent replicates). Thresholds of `2` and `3` are nominal and uncorrected for the many windows, rings, and conditions tested, and some windows were chosen after looking at the data, so `|z|` between `2` and `3` is suggestive only. For the current-noise statistic `W_C`, simulation shows that under reversible chains at the `W_C = 0` boundary `z` is centred near `−0.45` at `300 s`, so `z < −2` occurs up to about `7%` of the time by chance (`5%` at `30 min`), not `2.3%`. `FP` (false positive) counts a control that crossed the stated threshold.

## Layer 1 — Overview, Intuition & Scope

### The 30-Second Mental Model

Every system that changes over time—light fading from a molecule, ions crossing a membrane, particles diffusing through mucus, or a star cooling after an eruption—leaves behind a **decay curve** $y(t)$. How do you tell what is happening inside a noisy system **without** guessing a complex equation or running fragile curve-fitting software?

1. **The Everyday Intuition — Independent Lanes vs. a Multi-Step Assembly Line:**
   - **Independent Lanes (Passive Relaxation, $W \ge 0$):** Imagine people leaving a stadium through several independent exits at different speeds. At first the fast lanes empty rapidly, then the slower lanes linger. When you plot the remaining crowd on a logarithmic scale, **the curve bends upward like a bowl** (or forms a straight ramp if there is only one exit speed). Take three readings at equal time steps, `y₁, y₂, y₃`, and compute `W = y₁·y₃ − y₂²`. Any mixture of independent exponential decays with positive weights gives `W ≥ 0`, a bowl (Cauchy–Schwarz; Bernstein 1929 characterizes such mixtures).
   - **Multi-Step Assembly Line / Active Engine ($W < 0$):** Now imagine a two-step relay ($A \to B \to C$) where Stage B *must wait* for Stage A, or an engine actively burning fuel (such as ATP) to drive a directed loop. Output cannot peak immediately; it builds up and then drops, bending **downward like a dome** ($W < 0$). Because a passive mixture of independent decays never bends downward, **a dome rules that model out**. It does not say which alternative is present: a relay, a driven cycle, a compressed-exponential gel such as mucus, or an instrument artifact. The test is one-way: a bowl does not show that a system is passive.
2. **The Two Traps in Real-World Measurements:**
   - **Self-Noise Bias:** Squaring a noisy middle measurement $\hat{y}_2^2$ adds positive noise variance $\mathbb{E}[\eta^2]$ that gets subtracted in `W = y₁·y₃ − y₂²`, manufacturing a **false dome ($W < 0$)** on weak signals.
   - **Unknown Background Offset ($\delta_0$):** Uncalibrated detector dark counts or flat baseline offsets shift the curve and corrupt raw curvature.
3. **The 3-Part Closed-Form Recipe (No Curve Fitting):**
   - **`W_split` (A/B Stream Splitting):** Interleaves incoming events into two independent half-streams $A$ and $B$, computing $y_2^A y_2^B$ instead of $\hat{y}_2^2$ so self-noise bias vanishes ($\mathbb{E}[\hat{W}_{\text{split}}] = W$) under cross-half independence (exact for random Poisson thinning; for odd/even interleaving it relies on independent photon arrival times).
   - **`W_diff,split` (4-Point Difference Filter):** Takes adjacent differences $d_k = Y_k - Y_{k+1}$ before applying `W_split`, canceling any unknown constant background $\delta_0$ on raw counts without background subtraction.
   - **`H_3,diff` ($3 \times 3$ Rank Sieve):** Evaluates a closed-form $3 \times 3$ determinant to test whether the data require a third decay speed (at high counts; a null result means two suffice at the available signal-to-noise).

### End-to-End Pipeline

```text
   Raw Time-Series / Event Stream (Photons, Ion Currents, Scattering, Light Curves)
                                        │
                        ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                        ▼                               ▼
               Half-Stream A (Odd)             Half-Stream B (Even)
                        │                               │
                        └───────────────┬───────────────┘
                                        ▼
         Stage 1: Causal Sensor Dead-Zone & Truncation Gate (t ≥ t_safe)
                                        ▼
         Stage 2: 4-Point Adjacent Differencing: d_k = Y_k − Y_(k+1)
                  (Cancels an unknown constant baseline δ_0)
                                        ▼
         Stage 3: Split Cross-Curvature: W_diff,split = d1^A d3^B − d2^A d2^B
                  (Removes quadratic self-noise bias E[η²])
                                        ▼
         Stage 4: 3×3 Hankel Rank Sieve (H_3,diff) & Rate Spread (σ̂_λ,diff)
                  (Tests for a third mode in closed form, no fitting)
                                        │
            ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
            ▼                           ▼                           ▼
       W_diff > 0                  W_diff = 0                  W_diff < 0
      UPWARD BOWL                 STRAIGHT RAMP              DOWNWARD DOME
 ┌────────────────────┐      ┌────────────────────┐      ┌─────────────────────┐
 │ Multi-Rate Passive │      │ Single Exponential │      │ Sequential Cascade  │
 │ Mixture (Heterog.) │      │ + Flat Baseline    │      │ Active Cycle / Gel  │
 └────────────────────┘      └────────────────────┘      └─────────────────────┘

   Each label is "consistent with", not a diagnosis: a dome excludes a passive mixture
   but is also produced by instrument response, window truncation, and dead time;
   a bowl is required of a passive mixture but driven systems can also show one.
```

### Plain-English to Math Glossary

| Plain-English Concept | Scientific / Mathematical Term | Role in the Pipeline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Raw timestamp / signal log** | Photon microtime, dwell stream, or $g_2(\tau)$ | Input event stream partitioned into equal or unequal time windows |
| **Sensor warm-up blur** | IRF (Instrument Response Function) | Hardware distortion near $t = 0$ requiring a safe start offset (`≥ +2500 ps`) |
| **Upward "Bowl" ($W \ge 0$)** | Bernstein complete monotonicity / log-convexity | Required of any passive mixture of independent rates (`Var_w(λ) ≥ 0`); driven systems can also give a bowl at some lags, so a bowl does not show passivity |
| **Downward "Dome" ($W < 0$)** | Bateman serial cascade ($A \to B \to C$) or `PF₂` | Downstream stage waits for upstream stage, active cycle, compressed stress ($\beta > 1$), or an instrument artifact |
| **A/B half-stream cross-check** | Split-Sample Cross-Wronskian (`W_split`) | Eliminates $\mathbb{E}[\eta^2]$ self-noise squaring bias under cross-half independence |
| **4-point background canceller** | Adjacent-Difference Split Wronskian (`W_diff,split`) | Cancels unknown constant DC sensor pedestal ($\delta_0$) without subtraction |
| **Instant mode counter** | $3 \times 3$ Hankel-Turán determinant (`H_3,diff`) | Tests whether more than two decay populations are needed, in closed form |
| **Burst-length variability** | Squared coefficient of variation `CV²` (randomness parameter) | `CV² < 1` rules out a positive mixture of exponentials; used for event lists |
| **One-way driven loop** | Broken detailed balance | A driven cycle can give $W_C < 0$ (in simulation, with complex and with real eigenvalues); a reversible chain cannot. $W_C \ge 0$ does not show that a system is passive |

pooling raw durations from `10` patches of different speed cuts power to `57.3%`, while scoring each patch and combining the scores gives `>99.9%` (all `4,000` simulated runs). *Measured on public motor-protein dwells (Zenodo 5567245, F₁-ATPase, not `CFTR`):* at saturating `3 mM` ATP, `1,599` dwells give `CV² = 0.42` after dead-time adjustment (`0.37` raw) against a `1%` critical value of `0.89`, so a passive mixture is rejected; with a slowly hydrolysed ATP analog, `192` dwells give `0.81` (`p = 0.08`, not rejected); at binding-limited `300 nM` ATP, `443` dwells give `1.41`, significantly above the value of `1` expected for a single rate-limiting step (post hoc upper-tail `p = 0.0004`), which suggests heterogeneity such as differences between molecules; a passive mixture is not rejected there, and because the test is one-way that result shows nothing further. *Limits:* the simulation assumes ideal sequential steps, no misclassified bursts, and one channel per patch; a step-finding algorithm that misses short dwells can lower `CV²`; no passive-system control has been run on event lists, because the BK cache holds only truncated histograms; and no `CFTR` recording has been tested. `benchmark/whole_sample/run_on_patches.py` applies the dead-time rule and steps 3 to 5 of the fixed analysis to a table of burst durations (not the comparator).
- **Simulation Limits:** All macroscopic detection-time figures use a Gaussian (many-channel) approximation with the exact `C(τ)` from the rate matrix plus white noise; finite-`n` effects (so the `n = 1` and `n = 10` entries are indicative only), filtering, baseline drift, and rundown are not modelled.
- **False-Alarm Calibration (`benchmark/nullcal/`):** `0 / 2,000` checks the exact population sign only, so the noisy test was simulated under reversible chains with the same pipeline (`n = 100`, `K = 16`, one lag triple). On the least favourable reversible chain (`W_C = 0`), the null `z` is shifted negative, toward the signal: mean `−0.45` and `7.05%` of `40,000` runs below `−2` at `300 s` (`1.18%` below `−3`; other reversible chains range from `1.8%` to `7.2%` below `−2`); `4.9%` and `0.6%` at `30 min`. The shift comes from subtracting each block's own mean and shrinks with record length; the null is consistent with a shifted *t* distribution with `K − 1 = 15` degrees of freedom (KS `p = 0.19`), not with a shifted normal. With thresholds set on the most extreme of the three well-resolved reversible chains (smaller random-chain runs reached slightly more extreme quantiles), the listed driven cycle is detected `54%` of the time at `300 s` and `99.7%` at `30 min` for a `5%` false-alarm rate (`23%` and `97.3%` at `1%`). This is still the Gaussian many-channel limit with white noise.
- **Sign Is Not a Classifier:** In the same simulations, raising the forward drive of the listed cycle by `10× / 30× / 100×` lowers the fraction of lag triples with `W_C < 0` from `100%` to `82.7% / 67.6% / 72.9%`, and the real-eigenvalue driven cycle is negative on `100%` of lag triples yet too weak to detect in `2 h`. A driven system can therefore show a bowl at some lags, and complex eigenvalues are not required for a dome.

## Layer 3 — Technical Forensics, `LC-FCE` Pipeline, Governance & Appendix

### 1. Functional-Analytic Origin (Results A & B) & Three Mathematical Deductions

*In plain English: if a signal is a sum of independent decays with positive weights, its logarithm never curves downward (a single decay gives a straight line), so three equally spaced readings must satisfy `y₁·y₃ ≥ y₂²`.* By Cauchy–Schwarz (the forward half of **[Bernstein's 1929 characterization of completely monotone functions](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02592679)**) and the **Koppel (1972) / Frisken (2001) method of cumulants**, any observable `y(s) = ∫₀^∞ e^(−λs) dμ(λ)` with `dμ(λ) ≥ 0` satisfies `(d²/ds²) ln y(s) = Var_w(s)(λ) ≥ 0`, and hence `W(s₀, D) = y(s₀)·y(s₀+2D) − y(s₀+D)² ≥ 0` for every `s₀` and `D`. This single cone applies to:
- **Time-Domain Photonics (`TCSPC / FLIM / smFRET`):** $s = t$ microtime; $W < 0$ is consistent with serial `D* → A* → A₀` (Bateman 1910, *Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc.* 15:423; [Schoenberg 1951](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02790092)) or with uncalibrated IRF/truncation/baseline artifacts.
- **Correlation Spectroscopy (`FCS / DLS / XPCS`) & Patch-Clamp Noise:** $s = \tau$ lag; reversible Markov chains or Brownian polydispersity satisfy $W_C \ge 0$, whereas broken detailed balance or compressed stress relaxation ($\beta > 1$) can yield $W_C < 0$.
- **Porous Hydrogel Transport (`Brinkman` Mucus Sieves):** $s = z$ penetration depth across heterogeneous pore throats ($W \ge 0$; adding a positive Donnan $z^{-2}$ screening term keeps it so, `M16`), whereas saturable binding or active transport can yield $W < 0$ (not tested here).

**Results & Deductions:**
1. **`W_split` & Background Error Budget (Result B, `M20–M21`):** *In plain English: splitting an event stream into two interleaved halves removes the false downward bend caused by squaring noisy counts, while a formula for the average error shows how a background offset or slope shifts the result.* Naive unsplit Wronskians bias single-exponential curves negative at low counts (`−0.354·a₂²` at `N = 3×10⁴` vs. `+0.082·a₂²` symmetrized `W_split` and `+0.078·a₂²` `W_unsplit,bc`). Lean `M21` checks the Rademacher sign-flip identity; under cross-half independence (an assumption, exact for Poisson thinning), analysis and Monte Carlo give (`+8.1%`, `+0.9%`, `+0.4%` SD ratio at `N = 3×10⁴, 3×10⁵, 10⁶`; plug-in SE `98.8%` coverage / `2.2%` rejection at `N = 3×10⁴` vs. `97.5%` / `3.4%` exact bilinear SE), within-half covariance `Σ_A = diag(λ) + (M²/B)b·11ᵀ`, and **`𝔼[ΔW_split] = −δ₀(a₁ + a₃ − 2a₂) − ε(a₁ − a₃) − ε²`**.
2. **Unequal-Grid Cumulant Cone (Result A; lemmas `M13–M14`, `M16`, `M18`):** *In plain English: the same upward-bowl rule still holds even when measurements are taken at irregular, uneven time or depth intervals.* Lean `M14` shows that a single exponential has zero second divided difference on any grid and that barycentric log-convexity implies `D²[t₁, t₂, t₃] ln y ≥ 0`; that positive mixtures are barycentrically log-convex follows from Hölder's inequality and is derived on paper. Also covers Donnan `z⁻²` screening (`M16`) and Vandermonde–Hankel rank (`M18`).
3. **Deduction 1 — Wronskian Sign Trichotomy:** *In plain English: in the ideal model, the middle stage of a two-step relay bends downward across its entire timeline, and if a parallel leak is present, there is one predictable moment where the curve flips from downward dome to upward bowl.* `W_diff,ser(t₀, D) = −c² w₁ w₂ (e^(−λ₁D) − e^(−λ₂D))² e^(−(λ₁+λ₂)t₀) < 0` globally for all `t₀ ≥ 0` in `A → B → C` (even in the monotonic tail `t₀ > t_peak`), with a unique crossover `t_cross > 0` under parallel bleedthrough.
4. **Deduction 2 — Zero-Fit Rate-Dispersion `σ̂_λ,diff` ([Koppel 1972](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1678153)):** *In plain English: a single closed-form ratio gives a rough, fit-free estimate of how spread out the decay speeds are, in inverse nanoseconds; on the two-construct test mixtures the plain version came out 9–22% below a two-rate reference value and the background-free version 14–50% above it, so read it as an order-of-magnitude estimate.* `σ̂_λ,diff := (D Δt)⁻¹ √max(0, Ŵ_diff,split / (d₂ᴬ d₂ᴮ))` is meaningful only when `z_diff,split` is significant: on 1-dye controls the unclipped value is not zero (`1.62` and `2.18 ns⁻¹` at `600 s` and `1800 s`; `0.47` and `0.87 ns⁻¹` on donor-only bursts at `D = 60`), and it reads `0.0000` only where the `max` clips it or the `z_diff,split > 1.96` gate withholds it. Recomputed from the cached histograms (`benchmark/controls/gap1_sigma_lambda.json`), it gives `0.5327 / 0.4179 ns⁻¹` on `1a_1b_Mix` (unsplit `σ̂_λ = 0.3245 / 0.2848`; equal-weight two-rate reference `0.3555 ns⁻¹`), `0.5406 / 0.4612 ns⁻¹` on `2a_2b_Mix` (unsplit `σ̂_λ = 0.3566 / 0.3151`; reference `0.4058 ns⁻¹`), and `0.5603 / 0.4545 ns⁻¹` on 3-dye tissue. Earlier drafts quoted `0.2966 / 0.2583` and `0.3204 / 0.2907 ns⁻¹`; those values do not appear in any of the `20` ledger-window evaluations of the package's formula (both mixtures, five sub-ensembles, `D = 60, 75`) and are withdrawn. The reference is the half-gap between the two construct rates and ignores the donor-only population also present.
5. **Deduction 3 — `3×3` Hankel-Turán Rank Ladder `H₃,diff` ([Prony 1795](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k433661n/f25.item); [Karlin 1968](https://books.google.com/books?id=8c3vAAAAMAAJ)):** *In plain English: the determinant of a small $3 \times 3$ table of differences tests whether the data require a third decay speed; a null result means two suffice at the available signal-to-noise, not that a third is absent.* On `6` windows (`5` differences `d₁..d₅`), `H₃,diff` vanishes for `M ≤ 2` + flat baseline and is `> 0` for `M ≥ 3` positive-weight modes (its sign can differ when weights have mixed signs, as in a serial cascade): Rung 1 (`M_est = 1`, 1-dye `cFLIM`) `z_H3,diff ∈ [−1.43, +0.34]`; Rung 2 (`M_est = 2`, 2-construct smFRET) `z_H2,diff = +9.14 / +5.76` with `z_H3,diff = +1.35 / −0.21` (both files also contain donor-only molecules near `3.7 ns`, a third lifetime not resolved at this signal-to-noise); Rung 3 (`M_est ≥ 3`, 3-dye tissue) **`z_H3,diff = +6.58 / +7.25`** (`+3.84` at a third window).

### 3. The 5-Stage `LC-FCE` Screening Pipeline & 5 Use Cases

*In plain English: three gates screen out detector blur, clipped windows, and background drift; the curvature checks then run and are compared with a full likelihood fit.* Running **Stage 1 (Causal IRF/Filter Dead-Zone Gate `t ≥ t*`) → Stage 2 (Fixed-Anchor Truncation Gate `t₄ + M ≤ T_max`) → Stage 3 (Pre-Pulse vs. Tail Pedestal & `P²` Counter Gate) → Stage 4 (Joint `W_split`, `W_diff,split`, `H₃,diff`, `σ̂_λ,diff` Cross-Check) → Stage 5 (Profiled LRT / ML Comparator)** supports five use cases:
- **Lens 1 — Single-Channel Gating-Step Screen (`benchmark/whole_sample/cv2_burst_test.py`, `benchmark/single_channel_sample_size.py`):** For event lists the whole-sample variability test is the primary tool (`91.2%` power at `100` bursts in simulation for two steps of similar length, `32%` when one is five times faster; `benchmark/whole_sample/`); the three-bin screen below is kept for comparison. Model-light screen (`N = 1,036` A / `3,166` B for 80% power at `α = 0.01`; `N = 2,580` A / `7,886` B for `𝔼[z] = 5` on `50 ms` bins; `4,588` A nested survival) paired with ML/HMM dwell fitting (`specs/CFTR_RETROSPECTIVE_BENCHMARK_PROTOCOL.md`).
- **Lens 2 — Macroscopic Current-Noise Simulation & Real FCS Autocorrelation Stain (`W_C,U` & `W_C,diff,U`):** Experimental `Zenodo 1249497` (`z_jk = +5.19 / +5.79`) + simulated multi-channel patches (`~26–32 min` to `𝔼[z] = −5` for `n = 100` at `σ_w = 1 × i_single`).
- **Lens 3 — Zero-Fit FLIM-FRET Mixture Contrast & Tissue Superpixel Contrast:** `Zenodo 1249497` (two-species mixtures; the serial-cascade reading of `RED_DA` is not supported by the controls) and `Zenodo 7656540` (`512 × 512` 3-dye tissue).
- **Lens 4 — Hydrogel / Mucus & Synchrotron XPCS Relaxation Sign:** `Zenodo 17207776` (`D = 500 s` vs. `D = 20 s`) and `Zenodo 5533877` (`12/12` mucus gel `z = −7.06`).
- **Lens 5 — Hardware Pedestal & Drift Check:** Isolates `33.2 ppm` dye-free IRF pedestals and `P²` background drift (`Zenodo 14848839`).

- **No overclaiming of classical prior art:** Cumulant positivity ([Koppel 1972](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1678153); [Frisken 2001](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.40.004087)), fixed-interval differencing ([Guggenheim 1926](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786442608564083)), serial-cascade `PF₂` log-concavity (Bateman 1910; [Schoenberg 1951](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02790092)), and Hankel rank ([Prony 1795](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k433661n/f25.item); [Karlin 1968](https://books.google.com/books?id=8c3vAAAAMAAJ)) are classical. Two further ingredients are known in other fields under other names: replacing a squared noisy quantity by the product of two independent halves (cross-spectra between data splits in cosmology; two-detector pseudo-cross-correlation in light scattering and FCS), and determinant tests for the number of mixture components (Lindsay 1989, *Ann. Stat.* 17:722). Shape constraints on the autocovariance of reversible chains are used in Markov-chain Monte Carlo (Geyer 1992, *Stat. Sci.* 7:473; [Berg & Song 2023](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2335)). The variability statistic used for event lists is the classical randomness parameter of single-molecule kinetics ([Svoboda, Mitra & Block 1994](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.25.11782); [Schnitzer & Block 1995](https://doi.org/10.1101/sqb.1995.060.01.085); [Moffitt & Bustamante 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.12545)), and dwell-time properties of channels at equilibrium are treated by [Kijima & Kijima 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5193%2887%2980188-1); we cite these for the statistic and its setting, and did not re-read their full text for this note. No single ingredient here is new; a limited literature search did not find this combination, which is not the same as showing it is new. The noise-based test for driven cycles is also classical: current-fluctuation analysis ([Neher & Stevens 1977](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.bb.06.060177.002021); [Sigworth 1980](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1980.sp013426)), spectral positivity of reversible Markov chains (Kelly 1979, *Reversibility and Stochastic Networks*, Wiley), its use to detect broken detailed balance from time-series asymmetry in channel noise ([Steinberg 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-3495%2886%2983449-X)), later generalized to phase-space probability currents in active biological systems ([Battle et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac8167)), and irreversible ATP-driven `CFTR` gating ([Csanády, Vergani & Gadsby 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0911061107)). `W_split` requires cross-half independence and calibrated baselines; and under a correctly specified model, unbinned ML-LRT is substantially more efficient (`~100%` at `N = 300` vs. `29.5%` for 3-bin `D₂` at `α = 0.01`).
