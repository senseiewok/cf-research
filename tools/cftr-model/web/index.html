The CFTR protein in 3D, from four published structures

Draft, in review Research, not medical advice

CFTR is a human protein, and variants of it are linked to cystic fibrosis. Here are four published structures of it, each drawn as a smooth tube through the position of one atom in every amino acid it places. Turn them, switch between them, and see where amino acid 508 sits, and where it is absent in the two structures of CFTR with F508del (8EIQ and 8EJ1).

The positions of the points come from published structure files, not from a drawing; the tube that joins them is ours. What a structure cannot show is listed below.

    A guided look

    Each step sets the picture above. The words stay here whether or not the picture runs.

    1. A shape built from published data

      This is human CFTR, drawn as one point for each amino acid the model places, joined in order. The positions come from a model that scientists fitted to electron-microscope data (structure 6MSM, published in 2018). Drag to turn it, or use the sliders.

    2. Four marked parts of one chain

      CFTR is a single chain of 1,480 amino acids. UniProt marks two membrane-spanning parts (residues 81 to 365 and 859 to 1155) and two nucleotide-binding parts (423 to 646 and 1210 to 1443). It marks the long stretch from 654 to 831 as disordered, and none of that stretch is placed in the chain this page draws, in any of the four files. Three of the files (6MSM, 5UAK and 8EIQ) also hold a short separate fragment of 16 to 19 residues of unknown identity, whose place in the sequence is not assigned; 5UAK's file calls it the R domain. It is not drawn. Wherever a model leaves out a stretch, a dashed line shows only that it is not drawn.

    3. Position 508

      Residue 508 sits inside the first nucleotide-binding part. In structure 6MSM it is phenylalanine, one of the amino acids that make up proteins. The rose ring and dot show it.

    4. The same protein with 508 absent

      Structure 8EIQ is human CFTR carrying F508del, the loss of the amino acid at position 508. Its model runs from residue 507 straight to 509, and the empty ring marks the place midway between them. Its file also records an engineered change at position 1371 (glutamate replaced by glutamine), as do the files for 6MSM and 8EJ1; 5UAK does not have it. This structure was solved with the three medicines of Trikafta (the title in its file lists them) and with ATP and magnesium present. This page does not show where they sit or how they act.

    5. Laying the shapes over each other

      Here the membrane-spanning parts of each model are lined up on 6MSM, a computation made for this page. After that fit, the membrane-spanning parts of 8EIQ are 0.84 Å from those of 6MSM, as a root-mean-square distance over 566 residues (1 Å is a ten-billionth of a metre). The two dephosphorylated models, 5UAK and 8EJ1, are about 5 Å away (5.01 Å over 557 residues, and 4.87 Å over 547). 8EJ1 was solved at lower resolution (6.9 Å), so less detail is available for it. These numbers describe differences in shape between models. They do not say why, they do not say that any structure is open or closed, and they do not show what the medicines do.

    What this page does not show

    • What happens inside a living cell. A structure is a still picture of one arrangement of the protein. It cannot show what happens to the protein inside a cell, and it gives no count of how many channels reach the cell surface in anyone.
    • Where the pore and the gate are, how chloride passes through, or how any medicine acts. The sources we have checked so far do not settle these for this page, so we leave them out.
    • Anything about what a person with cystic fibrosis can expect, or about treatment. A care team is the place for that.
    • Everything in the chain. Each model places between 1,127 and 1,181 of the 1,480 positions in the UniProt sequence; the rest are not drawn.

    What the model leaves out

    • Only the alpha-carbon atom of each amino acid is drawn: no side chains, no other backbone atoms, no hydrogens.
    • Only chain A of model 1 of each entry is used.
    • A stretch a model does not place is not drawn. The dotted grey lines across it are straight connectors, not the shape.
    • The other molecules in an entry (ATP, magnesium, lipids, cholesterol, and in 8EIQ the three medicines) are not drawn. Their names appear only in the entry’s own title and ligand list.
    • The four entries were solved from different samples and under different conditions, at different resolutions. Differences between them are not a comparison of one experiment.
    • Residue numbers are the PDB entries’ author numbers, which equal UniProt’s numbers (checked residue by residue). Where a file declares a change from UniProt’s sequence it is shown in the table above.
    • There is no motion, no function and no mechanism in the picture.
