# Trial atlas: rule lexicon 0.3.2-draft

Status: proposed, written 2026-10-11. Implemented in `tools/trial_atlas/lexicon.json` as 0.3.2-draft, on the `atlas/frozen-census` branch, for review. It builds on 0.3.1-draft (`proposals/2026-10-11-lexicon-0.3.1-proposal.md`).

What changed:
- Two small additions to `tools/trial_atlas/lexicon.py`: a guard on description upgrades, and the fallback companions.
- No class was added, removed or renamed; the 37 class ids are unchanged.

Provenance: rules by `claude-opus-5-5`, from the errors of 0.3.1-draft on a labelled development set and from the class-level confusion counts of a second census set. No model was called to write the rules. Counts come from a throwaway script kept outside the repository, which tags each entry with `lexicon.py`'s own `tag_entry`. Clinical statements marked T0 are from memory and unverified.

## Data, and what was kept away

- **Development set.** 926 labelled primary-outcome entries: the 758 used for 0.3.1 plus 168 new ones. Each label is the class ids a model council gave. Every rule change was tuned and measured on these entries only.
- **Census-2 confusion counts.** Weighted counts by class for a fresh 992-row set, as counts only. They pointed to patterns; no entry of that set was read.
- **The snapshot.** It was read only to count entries that change class. Its matched spans were read where a pattern had to be found that the development set did not contain. This happened once: the mucociliary entry described under "Pharmacokinetics and mucociliary clearance".
- **Not seen.** The held-out half and its labels were never opened.
- **Untrusted text.** Registry wording was read as data. No registry sentence, trial id or sponsor name appears here or in the lexicon; examples and tests use made-up phrases.

All development counts are **in-sample**.

### Pharmacokinetics and mucociliary clearance

The census counts showed mucociliary-clearance entries tagged pharmacokinetics. On the snapshot the one such entry was an area under the curve of mucociliary clearance (abbreviated MCC). The PK rule that fired was PK-01's bare "area under the curve", not a clearance rule. No PK clearance rule matched a mucociliary entry.

| Rule | Change |
| --- | --- |
| PK-01 | Keeps only the explicit forms: the concentration-time curve, or a plasma, serum or blood curve. The glucose and insulin lookarounds added in 0.3.1 are no longer needed. |
| PK-04 (new) | The bare "area under the curve", moved here. It is weak (`suppressed_by_any_other_class`), so it is pharmacokinetics only when the entry names no other class. An area under the curve of glucose, FEV1, mucociliary clearance or gut water content is that measure. |
| PK-02, PK-05 (new) | The acronym AUC is moved out of PK-02 into PK-05, weak in the same way. PK-02 keeps Cmax, Tmax, half-life and the other acronyms. |
| MCC-02 (new) | MCC, case-sensitive. On the snapshot, every other use of it (8 measure or description fields) was already in an entry tagged mucociliary clearance. |
| MICRO-03 | Also gives way to PK-04 and PK-05. Two weak rules do not drop each other, and a sputum AUC is a drug level. Its word repetitions are bounded. |

### Gastrointestinal

| Rule | Change |
| --- | --- |
| GI-04 (new) | Two groups of measures: <ul><li>Intestinal inflammation: faecal or stool calprotectin; gut, intestinal or bowel inflammation.</li><li>Gastrointestinal symptom measures: irritable bowel syndrome, the IBS severity score, reflux or GERD symptoms, and the Rome questionnaire.</li></ul> |
| GI-05 (new) | IBS and IBS-SSS, case-sensitive. |
| GI-03 | Small-bowel or colonic water content (T0: an MRI gut measure). |

**Why GI-04 reverses a 0.3.0 decision.** 0.3.0-draft tagged faecal calprotectin as inflammation only. On the development set, the council gave both classes in nearly every case:
- Intestinal inflammation or faecal calprotectin: gastrointestinal plus inflammation in 5 of 6 entries.
- A gastrointestinal symptom questionnaire: gastrointestinal plus named instrument in 5 of 5 entries.

Both glosses apply, as for the SNOT-22 and sinus in 0.3.1. Calprotectin in the lungs or blood stays inflammation only.

### Patient-reported and feasibility

| Rule | Change |
| --- | --- |
| PRO-06 | A one-word name is accepted when the instrument's abbreviation follows in brackets, as in "Self-Example Questionnaire for Children (SEQ-C)". Only Questionnaire and Inventory are accepted in this form, not Scale or Index, so "Body Mass Index (BMI)" stays out. Every word slot is length-bounded. |
| PRO-04 | The Rome diagnostic questionnaire, which is also gastrointestinal through GI-04. |
| PRO-01 | PedsQL written "Peds QL" or "Peds QOL". |
| FEAS-01, FEAS-02 | "Usability", "ease of use" and "easy to use" removed. How easy a device or app is to use is a usability outcome, often a named scale, not whether a study or treatment can be carried out. The council called these named instruments or `other` (census counts and 2 development entries). Leaving the study "due to" or "because of" an adverse event is safety, not attrition. Acceptability stays feasibility; the council agrees on it. |

### Inflammation and sputum

| Rule | Change |
| --- | --- |
| INFL-01 | Elastase in sputum, airway or lavage samples, and free elastase, added beside neutrophil elastase. Faecal elastase stays gastrointestinal. "Neutrophil cell count" also added. |
| SPUT-01 | "Sputum wet and dry weight", when both are named. |

### Two declared mechanisms

**DU-02, a description upgrade.** The trigger:
- The measure gives only vague lung-function wording and a safety class, as in "tolerability measured by pulmonary function tests".
- Then the named lung-function rules are read on the description: FEV1, other spirometry, lung clearance index and lung mechanics.

A match replaces the vague tag, and the safety tag stays. `lexicon.py` gained one guard: an upgrade applies only when the measure actually gave a class the upgrade replaces. A safety-only measure therefore never gains a lung class this way.

**FC-01 and FC-02, fallback companions.** When the description decides, it normally keeps only its earliest match. With a companion group, the earliest match of each other class in the same group is kept too. The groups:
- **FC-01:** inflammation markers with oxidative-stress markers. These are two marker families named side by side, as the coordinator asked.
- **FC-02:** gastrointestinal with named instrument. This covers a gastrointestinal symptom measure and the questionnaire that scores it.

Both mechanisms are declared in `lexicon.json`, checked at load (unknown class or rule ids are refused), and tested.

## Timing

Every pattern was timed: rules, suppression patterns, the composite, not-stated and fallback-restriction patterns. That is 3,706 searches on hostile 20,000-character inputs. Inputs included runs of "PA-", "Abc-", "Abc Def ", "a ", "-", digits, "(", "Abc (ABC) ", "Rome ", "IBS-", "SNOT-" and "liver ".
- **Results:** the slowest search, taking the minimum of three runs, was 7.4 ms (GI-01, LMECH-01 and INFL-01). None was over 10 ms.
- **Linear growth:** doubling the input to 40,000 and then 80,000 characters doubled the time each step, so these are long alternations scanned linearly, not backtracking.
- **PRO-06:** on title-case runs it takes 2 ms at 20,000 characters.
- **The repository's test:** `test_every_pattern_is_fast_on_adversarial_text` passes.

Every rule changed or added here uses bounded word repetitions, and a test checks that their character classes are never repeated without a bound. Unchanged rules still contain short `\w*` suffixes (for example `pharmacokinetic\w*`). They time linearly and were not rewritten, so as not to touch 30 rules that do not need it.

## Left alone, and why

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| GI-01 against safety (the census's 7.5 weighted) | Every development case is one study that registers the same measure text about a dozen times. The council gave safety only to the entries whose descriptions list safety tests, and gastrointestinal plus safety to the rest. A rule cannot tell identical measures apart. |
| spirometry_other against fev1 (the census's 7 weighted) | In the development set, the council gives fev1 only in all 9 entries where the measure names FEV1 and the description lists other spirometry. No ratio or percent-predicted case was mislabelled. The rules follow the gloss, and the census pattern was not found in development data. |
| pro_unnamed's remaining false positives (6) | Each falls into one of these: <ul><li>A visual analogue scale: the gloss says it is unnamed.</li><li>Satisfaction: the council is split, with 2 entries unnamed and 1 feasibility.</li><li>An in-house questionnaire with a lower-case name.</li><li>A new unnamed three-item measure.</li><li>A diary card under a one-word brand name.</li><li>Lower-case scale names.</li></ul> Each would need words fitted to one entry. |
| healthcare_use misses (4) | They are: <ul><li>A "need for antibiotics" measure that the council counted as both exacerbation and healthcare use. A healthcare rule there would stop the description from giving the exacerbation, the trade-off the 0.3 proposal recorded.</li><li>An infection count whose description defines it partly by antibiotics and admission. The measure named another class, so the description is not read.</li><li>Two wordings that name no healthcare measure.</li></ul> |
| LUNGFN-V1's remaining false positives (3) | Two are vague measures whose descriptions name no measure, and one is adherence to a follow-up schedule. |
| SURV-01, SURV-04, LIVER-02 | One entry each, unchanged from 0.3.1. The council labelled near-identical wording differently. |
| MICRO-01 "microbiota" | A gut-microbiota entry the council called `other`; a question for the clinician reader. |
| oxidative_stress versus inflammation | Fixed through FC-01, not by narrowing OXS-01. The 0.3.1 OXS and MICRO rules were not taking entries that name only inflammation markers. The misses came from the earliest-match fallback and from sputum or elastase wording INFL-01 did not know. |

## Held-out measurement (added by the controlling agent after the rules were written)

The held-out half is 387 rows of the redrawn census frozen set's sampled strata whose entry id hashes to an odd number (SHA-1, first 8 hex digits, modulo 2); the rule writer never saw them. The development entries had already been excluded from those strata, so the sample was out-of-sample for 0.3.1 as well. Unweighted, pooled over the specific classes, rules plus the consensus-filtered model proposals, against the council's labels (`other` and `not_stated` not scored):

| Lexicon | True positives | False positives | Misses | Precision | Recall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.3.1-draft | 364 | 48 | 50 | 0.883 | 0.879 |
| 0.3.2-draft | 367 | 45 | 47 | 0.891 | 0.886 |

In-sample on the 926 development entries the same figures went 0.928 and 0.886 to 0.941 and 0.910. The held-out gain is small. Per class on the held-out half: `pro_named_other` 27 right and 15 missed became 29 and 13, `pro_unnamed` false positives fell from 18 to 16, `feasibility_adherence` false positives from 9 to 7, `mucociliary_clearance` gained its one missed entry; `gi_pancreatic` got worse (5 false positives became 7). These are measurements against model-council labels (tier T2). The controlling agent adopted 0.3.2 after seeing the pooled held-out figures, which is a yes or no decision and not a tuning step; the next frozen draw excludes the development entries again.
