# Trial atlas: rule lexicon 0.3.0-draft

Status: proposed, written 2026-10-11. Implemented in `tools/trial_atlas/lexicon.json` as 0.3.0-draft, on a branch, for review. The maintainer approved extending the lexicon. **The nine added classes, and the clinical choices listed below, need a clinician reader before anything built on them is published.**
Provenance: analysis and rules by `claude-opus-5-5` (the build agent), from aggregate counts over a development set of outcome entries. No model was called to write the rules. The counts below were computed by a throwaway script that applies the lexicon with `lexicon.py`'s own functions; they are not committed, because the entries are registry text.

## What this is answering

A first real run of lexicon 0.2.3-draft left 21.5 percent of 2,052 in-scope primary-outcome entries unclassified, above the 15 percent the gate allows (stop rule S2). A local model, held to exact quotes, proposed classes for most of the remainder, and a clinician-role review of the rules listed missing endpoint families and rules that match too much. This note decides, family by family, what to add, what to extend and what to leave alone.

## The data used, and what was kept away

- **Development entries only.** 2,002 in-scope entries: the first run's entries minus the 50 rows of the frozen set, which was drawn and labelled blind before this work. The rules were written and measured on the development entries only. Nobody working on the rules saw the frozen rows, their labels or their tags.
- **What each development entry carried.** Its wording, the 0.2.3 rule classes, and for unclassified entries either a model-proposed class with a verified quote (255 entries) or a short free-text family label the model gave when it found no class (160 entries).
- **Untrusted text.** Registry wording was read as data. No registry sentence, trial id or sponsor name is reproduced here or in the rules; rule examples are made-up or short generic phrases.

## Rule of thumb used

Add a class only when at least 5 development entries support it and it is a distinct measurement concept. Otherwise extend an existing class, or leave the entries to `other` (a model or a person assigns that, with a quote). The number of studies is given beside each count, because a single study can register several entries for one family. The count is necessary, not sufficient. Every new class that the atlas shows needs labelled positives in the frozen set, or stop rule S1 trips. So a family that meets the count but comes from few studies, or that needs a clinical framing first, was left out and is listed below. Unlike concepts are never merged; in particular pancreatic measures, symptoms and direct CFTR function stay in separate classes (`cf-research-context`, CFTR-related measures).

## Decisions: new classes

Counts are development entries tagged by 0.3.0-draft, with the number of distinct studies, and where they were before.

| Class id | Domain | Label | Gloss (as in the lexicon) | Entries (studies) | Before |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lung_mechanics` | pulmonary | Airway resistance and lung volumes | Airway resistance or reactance (oscillometry, plethysmography) or lung volumes such as residual volume, functional residual capacity or tidal volume. | 17 (12) | 14 unclassified, 2 lung function unspecified, 1 feasibility |
| `oxygenation` | pulmonary | Blood oxygen and carbon dioxide | Oxygen saturation, pulse oximetry, blood gases or transcutaneous carbon dioxide. | 16 (14) | 14 unclassified, 2 safety |
| `bone_density` | bone (new) | Bone mineral density | Bone mineral density or content, usually measured by DXA, or osteoporosis or osteopenia defined by it. | 8 (7) | 7 unclassified, 1 imaging |
| `mucociliary_clearance` | airway_mucus (new) | Mucociliary clearance | How fast the airways clear inhaled radiolabelled particles: mucociliary or cough clearance. | 17 (15) | 13 unclassified, 3 imaging, 1 feasibility |
| `sputum_properties` | airway_mucus (new) | Sputum amount and properties | How much sputum is expectorated (weight or volume), or its physical properties such as viscoelasticity. Not microbiology. | 22 (20) | 18 microbiology, 4 unclassified |
| `molecular_expression` | molecular (new) | Gene expression and methylation | Gene expression, RNA or DNA methylation measured in cells or blood, other than CFTR expression (that is a CFTR biomarker). | 11 (6) | 11 unclassified |
| `oxidative_stress` | molecular (new) | Oxidative stress markers | Markers of oxidative stress or redox balance, such as isoprostanes, nitrotyrosine or glutathione redox state. | 8 (8) | 7 unclassified, 1 safety |
| `physical_activity` | physical_function (new) | Physical activity | Everyday physical activity, such as steps or active minutes measured by an accelerometer or activity tracker. | 10 (9) | 9 unclassified, 1 patient-reported |
| `muscle_strength` | physical_function (new) | Muscle strength | Strength or endurance of limb muscles (grip, dynamometry) or breathing muscles (maximal inspiratory or expiratory pressure). | 17 (11) | 17 unclassified |

New domains and their labels: `bone` "Bone health"; `airway_mucus` "Mucus and mucociliary clearance"; `molecular` "Molecular and oxidative markers"; `physical_function` "Physical activity and muscle strength". Domain and class labels are drafts for the same clinician reader.

Why these are separate classes:
- **Sputum amount is not microbiology.** Most sputum-weight and rheology entries were tagged microbiology in 0.2.3, through the weak word "sputum" in the description. They measure airway clearance treatments, not organisms.
- **Oscillometry and lung volumes are not spirometry.** The model placed them in "other spirometry" because no closer class existed. They are different tests, so they get their own class rather than widening the spirometry gloss.
- **Muscle strength joins limb and breathing muscles.** Both measure muscle strength; only the muscle group differs. A clinician may prefer to split breathing-muscle strength into the pulmonary domain; there are 7 such entries.
- **Oxidative stress is kept apart from inflammation.** The model put two of these entries under inflammation markers. They measure redox balance, not inflammatory cells or cytokines.

The "never infer FEV1 from vague wording" rule is unchanged, and its test still passes.

## Generic titles and the description fallback

The lexicon reads the description only when the measure gives no class (or only vague wording), and keeps the earliest match there. Of 164 development entries whose measure starts with a generic title such as "change from baseline" or "number of participants", 0.2.3 classified 122 and 0.3.0-draft classifies 146. Most generic titles carry the measure later in the same title, so the measure decides; the description decided 8. Of the 18 still unclassified, 11 have an empty description. The fallback needed no change.

**Reading every changed entry against its text** (the build agent's reading, not a clinician's):

- **Improvements (44).** 17 of the 18 sputum-amount moves. The calprotectin and SNOT-22 single tags. The mucociliary-clearance entries that were tagged imaging only for the technique. The ratio entries. The oscillometry entries. An oro-caecal transit time measured by MRI. An isoprostane marker that was safety only through the word urinalysis. A nasal TEPD entry. A named sleep index. A hearing-test entry that was exacerbations only through a description word.
- **Arguable (4).**
  - Two oxygen-saturation entries whose descriptions frame them as safety. The measure names oxygen saturation, and the measure decides.
  - An activity scale filled in by the participant, now physical activity rather than patient-reported unnamed.
  - A C-peptide area-under-the-curve entry that gains glucose but keeps the old pharmacokinetics tag from "area under the curve" (an existing limit of PK-01).
- **Looks wrong (2).**
  - An exacerbation-rate entry whose description lists several outcomes moved from microbiology to patient-reported unnamed. Both tags are wrong: the measure is a bare "exacerbation rate", which stays unclassified by design.
  - A sputum-sample-adequacy entry is now sputum amount (the 18th sputum move).
  Neither can be fixed by a rule without over-fitting one study's wording.

**Found wrong during development and fixed before this count:**
- A bacterial-density entry in expectorated sputum went to sputum amount. "Expectorated sputum" alone no longer counts as amount, and MICRO-01 accepts "spp." after an organism.
- A bowel-movement symptom score was tagged gastrointestinal. Only bowel-movement frequency counts now.
- "Chloride transport" without a named test took entries whose descriptions name nasal potential difference. It is no longer a rule, so the description decides.
- An absorptive-clearance entry was read as drug clearance. "Clearance of a ..." is excluded.

Safety subtypes on the development entries after the change: hearing 4, physical examination 25.

## What this changes for the gate (to decide before relying on 0.3.0-draft)

- **The frozen set was labelled with the 0.2.3 class list.** An entry the labeller called `other` that 0.3.0-draft now tags with a new class will count against the rules' precision. A new class with no labelled positive in the frozen set trips stop rule S1 if it is shown. Before the gate is run with this lexicon, the person who labels must review the frozen rows labelled `other` against the new class list, blind to the rules' tags. Or the new classes must stay out of what is shown until the set is extended toward 150 rows, with oversampling of rare classes, as the design allows.
- **Class count.** The lexicon now has 37 classes (35 a rule can assign, plus `other` and `not_stated`). The grounding tool on another branch checks that its targets cover every class; it needs targets for the nine new ids.
- **The negative control.** No new class is marked CF-specific or `negative_control`. Mucociliary clearance and sputum properties are common in CF trials but are not CF-specific measures.

## What a clinician reader should check

- The nine new classes: names, glosses and domains.
- Muscle strength joining limb and breathing muscles.
- Sit-to-stand as exercise capacity rather than strength.
- FeNO as an inflammation marker.
- Oximetry inside a safety outcome counting as safety.
- Hearing tests as safety. A trial that aims to protect hearing would measure hearing as efficacy.
- The FEV1/FVC ratio as other spirometry.
- SNOT-22 as a named instrument only.
- Faecal calprotectin as inflammation only.
- "Liver function" as a safety laboratory measure.
