# Trial atlas: rule lexicon 0.3.0-draft

Status: proposed, written 2026-10-11. Revised the same day after two blind reviews: an adversarial rules review and a clinician-role review (a model in that role, not a clinician). Implemented in `tools/trial_atlas/lexicon.json` as 0.3.0-draft, on a branch, for review. The maintainer approved extending the lexicon. **The nine added classes, and the clinical choices listed below, need a clinician reader before anything built on them is published.**

Provenance: analysis and rules by `claude-opus-5-5` (the build agent), from aggregate counts over a development set of outcome entries. No model was called to write the rules. The counts below were computed by a throwaway script that applies the lexicon with `lexicon.py`'s own functions; they are not committed, because the entries are registry text. Clinical statements marked T0 are from memory and unverified.

## What this is answering

A first real run of lexicon 0.2.3-draft left 21.5 percent of 2,052 in-scope primary-outcome entries unclassified, above the 15 percent the gate allows (stop rule S2). A local model, held to exact quotes, proposed classes for most of the remainder, and a clinician-role review of the rules listed missing endpoint families and rules that match too much. This note decides, family by family, what to add, what to extend and what to leave alone.

## The data used, and what was kept away

- **Development entries only.** 2,002 in-scope entries: the first run's entries minus the 50 rows of the frozen set, which was drawn and labelled blind before this work. The rules were written and measured on the development entries only. Nobody working on the rules saw the frozen rows, their labels or their tags. The two reviews used the same development entries and synthetic phrases.
- **What each development entry carried.** Its wording, the 0.2.3 rule classes, and for unclassified entries either a model-proposed class with a verified quote (255 entries) or a short free-text family label the model gave when it found no class (160 entries).
- **Untrusted text.** Registry wording was read as data. No registry sentence, trial id or sponsor name is reproduced here or in the rules; rule examples are made-up or short generic phrases.

## Rule of thumb used

Add a class only when at least 5 development entries support it and it is a distinct measurement concept. Otherwise extend an existing class, or leave the entries to `other` (a model or a person assigns that, with a quote). The number of studies is given beside each count, because a single study can register several entries for one family.

The count is necessary, not sufficient. Every new class that the atlas shows needs labelled positives in the frozen set, or stop rule S1 trips. So a family that meets the count but comes from few studies, or that needs a clinical framing first, was left out and is listed below.

Unlike concepts are never merged. In particular, pancreatic measures, symptoms and direct CFTR function stay in separate classes (`cf-research-context`, CFTR-related measures).

Rules are written as positive forms of the measure (what is measured, with its context word), not as a word plus a growing list of exclusions. The second review showed that exclusion lists leak.

**New domains and their labels:**
- `bone`: "Bone health". Fractures and bone-turnover markers are not tagged.
- `airway_mucus`: "Mucus and mucociliary clearance".
- `molecular`: "Molecular and oxidative markers".
- `physical_function`: "Physical activity and muscle function".

**Labels renamed in the second round:**
- The pulmonary domain is now "Lung function and gas exchange", because it now holds blood-gas measures.
- The microbiology domain and its class (id `microbiology_sputum`, unchanged) are now "Microbiology", because sputum amount has its own class.
- The safety subtype `hearing` (id unchanged) is labelled "Hearing and balance", since vestibular tests are matched. Every subtype now has a label (`safety_subtype_labels`).

Domain and class labels are drafts for the clinician reader.

**Why these are separate classes:**
- **Sputum amount is not microbiology.** Most sputum-weight and rheology entries were tagged microbiology in 0.2.3, through the weak word "sputum" in the description. They measure airway clearance treatments, not organisms. The class pools amount (a treatment outcome) with rheology (a mechanism outcome); the clinician reader may want them apart.
- **Oscillometry, compliance and lung volumes are not spirometry.** The model placed them in "other spirometry" because no closer class existed. They are different tests, so they get their own class rather than widening the spirometry gloss.
- **Tidal volume is not in `lung_mechanics`.** It is a breathing-pattern or ventilator measure (clinician-role review), and 2 development entries are left unclassified.
- **Muscle strength joins limb and breathing muscles.** Both measure strength or endurance; only the muscle group differs. A limb-or-breathing facet would let the 7 breathing-muscle entries be shown apart; that is a later step.

**Decisions on placement, kept after the clinician-role review:**
- **`oxidative_stress` stays in the `molecular` domain.** The review suggested moving it under inflammation, because oxidant burden in CF is tied to neutrophilic inflammation (T0). It stays apart: the entries measure redox balance, not inflammatory cells or cytokines, and the move would change the inflammation domain's meaning. The reader can revisit this.
- **`exercise_capacity` stays in its own domain.** The review noted that exercise capacity, physical activity and muscle strength read oddly across two domains. Moving it would change an existing domain that the frozen set was labelled under, so it stays. The note in the lexicon says so.

## Decisions: over-matching rules fixed

Each fix has a test showing that the old false positive is gone and the true positive remains (`test_lexicon.py`, `Lexicon030Test` and `Lexicon030Round2Test`). Changed rules keep their ids and carry a `changed` note; the versioning rule is written in `lexicon.json` and the README.

| --- | --- | --- |
| GLU-01 | "insulin" matched insulin-like growth factor; bare "diabetes" matched a population; "glucose" matched glucose-6-phosphate | "insulin" not followed by "-like"; diabetes only as an outcome: incidence, onset, diagnosis, time to, developing, diabetes-free and the like, with modifiers such as type 1, type 2 or CF-related. Never "with (a diagnosis of) diabetes". The first round's one-word slot lost "incidence of type 2 diabetes" and "time to diabetes"; the second round restored them. |
| FEAS-01, FEAS-02 | "uptake" (FDG, tracer), "retention" (CO2, drug in the lung), "compliance" (lung, thorax), "recruitment" (neutrophils, leukocytes) | Positive forms only. | SURV-01 | "Exacerbation-free survival", "survival free of exacerbation" | No longer survival. Transplant-free survival still is, through the transplant rule. |

**Rules added in this draft and narrowed in the second round:**
- **PK-03, drug clearance.**
  - Removed: the open "clearance of X" with a list of exclusions, which tagged organisms, "the infection" and "symptoms" as pharmacokinetics.
  - Now positive forms only: plasma, serum, total, renal, systemic or apparent clearance; an antibiotic or modulator name before or after "clearance" (by drug-class suffix, plus colistin and the cephalosporin prefix); "clearance of the (study) drug"; or "clearance of X" followed by a plasma or dosing context.
  - The single-drug stems and the tracer exclusions fitted to one entry are gone.
- **OXS-01, glutathione.** It counts only when measured (a level, concentration, ratio, redox state, GSH/GSSG, or glutathione in a named sample). Glutathione given as a treatment (inhaled, nebulised, intravenous) is not a marker.
- **CFTRBIO-02, sweat secretion.**
  - It counts only with a stimulation or CFTR context (beta-adrenergic, cholinergic, methacholine, CFTR-dependent).
  - A bare sweat rate, sweat secretion, evaporimetry or skin water loss does not count.
  - "Chloride transport" alone names no test, so the description decides.
- **LMECH-01/02, lung mechanics.**
  - Resistance and reactance need a lung or airway word.
  - Residual volume is the lung volume unless the text names a device, a cup or the stomach. "Residual volume" is the standard name of a lung volume, and the non-lung uses carry their own qualifier.
  - R5, X5 and similar need "Hz" or the "rs" form.
  - Image-based airway resistance and its CT abbreviation were dropped; plethysmographic sRaw stays.
  - Low- or high-frequency resistance is accepted (oscillometry wording).
- **SPUT-01, sputum.**
  - Sputum amount needs a sputum word with weight, volume, amount or mass, or a unit; this replaces a pattern fitted to one entry.
  - Viscoelasticity and rheology need a sputum or mucus word; blood rheology is not sputum.
  - "Sputum quantity", which in development was about sample adequacy, is not amount.
  - Percent solids is included.
- **NPD-04.** TEPD on cultured or primary cells is not the nasal test.
- **STR-02.** MEP counts only next to MIP, because MEP alone is also a motor evoked potential. SNIP, knee-extensor and isokinetic strength were added.
- **GI-03.** Only how often bowel movements happen counts, not a symptom score about them.
- **SAFE-KIDNEY.** Outcome forms only; "decreased renal function" describing who was enrolled does not count.
- **SAFE-VITAL2.** The temperature of a solution is not body temperature.
- **BONE-01/02.**
  - Osteoporosis, osteopenia and low bone density count only as outcomes.
  - Densitometry alone is not enough; body composition by densitometry is nutrition.
  - aBMD, vBMD and the trabecular bone score were added.
  - "Bone mineral apparent density" is kept: it is the standard size-adjusted measure in children (T0).
- **EXER-03.** The timed up-and-go test was removed. It is a mobility and balance test, now left to `other`.

**The new safety rules never decide from the description.** SAFE-PE, SAFE-VITAL2, SAFE-LAB3, SAFE-KIDNEY, SAFE-KIDNEY2 and SAFE-HEARING are in `fallback_excluded_rules`, as SAFE-UNSPEC is, because a description that mentions a physical examination in passing should not make an entry a safety outcome.

**Oximetry inside a safety outcome (changed in the second round).** In the first round, the whole safety class suppressed oxygenation, so the bare word "safety" removed an oxygenation tag. Now:
- Oxygen saturation, oximetry or a blood gas named explicitly is tagged oxygenation. When a safety rule also matches, the entry keeps its safety tag too.
- Only the bare word "desaturation" (new rule OXY-03) gives way to safety, for example a desaturation adverse event or bronchospasm defined by desaturation.

**Text folding.** Before matching, subscript digits and the dash characters (hyphen, non-breaking hyphen, figure dash, en dash, em dash, minus sign) are folded one to one to ASCII digits and "-". "SpO₂", "FEV₁/FVC" and "insulin–like" are therefore read like their plain forms. Spans still map to the exact original text, which a test checks.

**The "never infer FEV1 from vague wording" rule** is unchanged, and its test still passes.

## Generic titles and the description fallback

The lexicon reads the description only when the measure gives no class (or only vague wording), and keeps the earliest match there. Of 164 development entries whose measure starts with a generic title such as "change from baseline" or "number of participants", 0.2.3 classified 122 and 0.3.0-draft classifies 145. Most generic titles carry the measure later in the same title, so the measure decides; the description decided 7. Of the 19 still unclassified, 11 have an empty description.

**One fallback fix.** A measure that names an exacerbation without the context EXAC-01 needs, such as "exacerbation rate", is left unclassified on purpose. In 0.2.3 and the first round, its description could still decide the tag from another outcome it listed. That happened in 5 of 6 such development entries: quality of life, cultures, a spirometry word, pancreatic insufficiency, antibiotic treatment. A fallback restriction (`matching.fallback_restrictions`, FB-01) now lets such a description confirm the exacerbations class but give no other class.

The generic alternative was to stop reading generic patient-reported wording in descriptions. It was not taken: that wording decides 12 other development entries, most of them correctly (a measure such as "self-esteem" with "questionnaire" in its description).

**Two small mechanisms were added to `lexicon.py`:**
- `suppressed_by_pattern`: a rule is dropped when a pattern also matches the same field. It is used so that a field naming absorptive (DTPA) clearance does not give mucociliary clearance.
- `fallback_restrictions`: described above.

Both load through the same risky-pattern check as every rule.

## Reviewer phrases

The adversarial review wrote 214 synthetic phrases, each with an expected class, and its script fails 91 of them against the first round. Against this revision it fails 6:
- "Vital signs including oxygen saturation" now also gets oxygenation. This is the decision on explicit oximetry above.
- "Lung function in patients receiving oxygen saturation monitoring" gets oxygenation beside the vague lung-function tag. The phrase names oxygen saturation, so this is accepted.
- "Liver function, assessed by fibrosis score" is safety, not liver. This follows the decision on liver function; a fibrosis-score exception would need the clinician reader.
- "Nasal nitric oxide" is not tagged. Nasal NO is a different test from exhaled NO, and no development entry used it.
- "Bowel habits" is not tagged; the phrase names no measure.
- "Xenon washout" is tagged imaging, not lung clearance index. Xenon is mostly an imaging gas in this set, and a xenon washout entry would be rare; this is left as is.

**Not now (each needs a separate decision):**
- **A CFQ-R respiratory-domain facet.** The CFQ-R domain most often registered is the respiratory one (T0, clinician-role review). A facet needs a new counts flag, which touches the page generator's whitelist.

## What this changes for the gate (to decide before relying on 0.3.0-draft)

- **The frozen set was labelled with the 0.2.3 class list.** An entry the labeller called `other` that 0.3.0-draft tags with a new class will count against the rules' precision. A new class with no labelled positive in the frozen set trips stop rule S1 if it is shown. Before the gate is run with this lexicon, either:
  - the person who labels reviews the frozen rows labelled `other` against the new class list, blind to the rules' tags; or
  - the new classes stay out of what is shown until the set is extended toward 150 rows, with oversampling of rare classes, as the design allows.
- **Class count.** The lexicon has 37 classes: 35 that a rule can assign, plus `other` and `not_stated`. The grounding tool on another branch checks that its targets cover every class; it needs targets for the nine new ids.
- **The negative control.** No new class is marked CF-specific or `negative_control`. Mucociliary clearance and sputum properties are common in CF trials, but they are not CF-specific measures.

## What a clinician reader should check

- The nine new classes: names, glosses and domains, especially oxygenation, lung mechanics without tidal volume, and the muscle-strength label.
- The placement of oxidative stress (molecular, not inflammation) and of exercise capacity (its own domain), and the renamed microbiology domain.
- Sit-to-stand as exercise capacity, and the timed up-and-go test left out.
- The FeNO wording in the inflammation gloss.
- Oximetry inside a safety outcome carrying both tags.
- Hearing tests as safety. A trial that aims to protect hearing would measure hearing as efficacy.
- "Liver function" as a safety laboratory measure, given CF liver disease trials that use liver tests as efficacy.
- Low bone density and z-score terminology for children.
- The FEV1/FVC ratio as other spirometry; SNOT-22 as a named instrument only; faecal calprotectin as inflammation only.
- Whether the CFQ-R respiratory facet and the ppFEV1 and exacerbation splits are needed before any public count.
- That nothing on a public page implies a measure is validated or preferred.
