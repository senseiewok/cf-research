# Evidence Corpus for Rare-Genotype Trial Screening & Class-I Registry Denominators (2026)

Section 1: Multi-Registry Modulator Ineligibility Denominators
Overall, 92.3 percent of the total CF population in the Registry was eligible by CFTR genotype for at least one CFTR modulator as of 2024.
In the 2024 CFF Patient Registry, 8.0 percent of individuals with known CFTR genotypes were ineligible by genotype for all FDA-approved CFTR modulators.
In 2024, 26,277 of the 29,314 (89.6 percent) people eligible for a CFTR modulator based on age and genotype had at least one prescription reported in the Registry.
The number of eligible individuals who were not prescribed a CFTR modulator fell from 3,065 (10.8 percent) in 2023 to 3,037 (10.4 percent) in 2024.

Of the 2,326 adults in the Australian registry in 2025 who had known eligibility for a modulator, 5.8 percent were not eligible for a modulator and 85.6 percent were eligible and prescribed a modulator.
Of 1,473 children and adolescents with known eligibility in the Australian registry in 2025, 170 children and adolescents (11.5 percent) were not eligible for a modulator.
We adopted the 2024 eligibility criteria of the European Medicines Agency for all countries in the ECFSPR except Israel, the Russian Federation and Switzerland.

Section 2: ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 PRISMA Screening and Word-Boundary Disambiguation
In the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 CF snapshot, 1,053 studies were identified through the condition query and 940 interventional studies were eligible after rules X1 through X6.
Across the 940 eligible interventional studies, 28 studies matched naive substring search for short genetic therapy acronyms before word-boundary disambiguation, including 19 false-positive matches.
Specifically, study NCT00846781 evaluated inhalation of denufosol tetrasodium and matched naive substring search only because the chemical name tetrasodium contains the three-letter substring aso.
Similarly, study NCT00909532 evaluated ivacaftor in subjects aged 12 years and older with the G551D mutation and serves as a negative control for Class I genetic therapy screening.
Enforcing strict regular expression word boundaries on short acronyms reduced false positives from 19 to 0 and identified 9 true rare-genotype genetic therapy trials across the 940 studies.
Against the 25-study labeled ClinicalTrials.gov genotype-criteria benchmark containing 20 positive rare-genotype or anti-infective studies and 5 negative controls, the screener achieved 1.0 precision, 1.0 recall, and 1.0 accuracy across all 25 studies.

Section 3: Matched Class-I, Splice-Modulation, and Genotype-Agnostic Clinical Trials
Study NCT05248230 AEROW evaluates aerosolized 4D-710 AAV gene therapy encoding human CFTR Delta-R in adults with cystic fibrosis who are ineligible for or intolerant of CFTR modulators.
Phase 1/2 clinical efficacy results for study NCT05248230 are not stated in the ClinicalTrials.gov registry record as of the 2026 snapshot.
Study NCT05712538 evaluates inhaled ARCT-032 mRNA therapy formulated in lipid nanoparticles in healthy adult subjects and adults with cystic fibrosis.
Study NCT06429176 evaluates SPL84 antisense oligonucleotide splice-modulation therapy in patients with cystic fibrosis carrying the 3849+10kb C-to-T splicing mutation.
Posted clinical efficacy results for study NCT06429176 are not stated in the ClinicalTrials.gov registry record as of the 2026 snapshot.
Study NCT04135495 evaluated ELX-02 translational readthrough therapy in cystic fibrosis subjects carrying at least one G542X nonsense allele or genetically similar Class I nonsense mutation.
