# Cystic Fibrosis Foundation goals: 2026–2027 in the 2026–2030 plan

**Research note, checked 2026-10-03.** This page summarizes public Cystic Fibrosis Foundation (CFF) material. It is not a statement on behalf of CFF, a forecast of outcomes, or medical guidance. Claim labels record that 2026-10-03 source check; the pages have not been re-checked since.

## What the public sources establish

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-strategic-plan] CFF's published strategic-plan page identifies a **five-year plan for 2026–2030**, organized around the Foundation's three mission areas: **Cure, Care, and Community**. That places 2026 and 2027 within the plan period. The public page gives this high-level structure but does not set out separate annual goal lists for 2026 and 2027. The linked PDF could not be text-extracted in this review, so its detailed contents remain unverified here.

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/annual-report] The 2025 annual report describes the plan as informed by community input and analysis of CFF's expertise and resources. It reports progress and funded work during 2025. Those examples help explain the direction of travel, but they are **2025 activities**, not proof of a 2026 or 2027 commitment.

## Community input informing the plan

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/deeper-dive-strategic-plan-survey-results] CFF reports that nearly 2,000 people responded to its Long-Term Strategic Plan Input Survey. The survey page says its results informed the 2026–2030 plan. Respondents rated these Foundation activities very important or absolutely essential over the following 3–5 years:

| Activity | Respondents selecting very important or absolutely essential |
| --- | ---: |
| Fund research and development for a cure | 97% |
| Fund research and drug development for CF complications | 96% |
| Advocate for access to high-quality, specialized care | 91% |
| Fund CF care and support the CF care model | 90% |
| Provide resources to navigate life with CF | 84% |
| Address barriers that lead to health disparities in CF | 75% |

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/deeper-dive-strategic-plan-survey-results] The survey's reported leading research priorities were genetic therapies, digestive complications, aging with CF, mental health, and anti-infectives or infection detection. These are **survey priorities**, not annual deliverables or a ranked CFF budget.

## 2025 activity: context, not a promise

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/annual-report] The 2025 annual report describes work across the three mission areas. Examples include advancing genetic-therapy research and other treatment approaches; studying longer-term effects and treatment burden; addressing care needs as more people with CF age; and supporting community navigation, connection, and advocacy. These are summaries of reported 2025 work, not a prediction that a specific program will continue unchanged through 2027.

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/annual-report] The annual report also describes work on treatments for people who cannot take modulators, infection detection and treatment, newborn screening, remote-care approaches, and access to specialized care. Each is an area to investigate further, not evidence that the lab should build a clinical tool or handle patient data.

## Research implications for this repository

- Treat Cure, Care, and Community as CFF's published organizing areas, not as permission to speak for the Foundation.
- Use the survey findings to identify questions for public-source research. Do not present respondent percentages as population estimates or as CFF's commitments.
- Keep research tools non-clinical unless a qualified partner, approved data environment, and appropriate governance are in place. This repository must not contain patient-identifiable data.
- For any future claim about a specific therapy, eligibility, approval, or care recommendation, follow the control repo's `cf-research-context` skill and cite the relevant primary source.
