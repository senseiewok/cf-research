# Cystic Fibrosis — reference

Grounding for this workspace. Keep claims tied to the cited source; update when the Foundation publishes changes.

## What CF is

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/intro-cf] Cystic fibrosis (CF) is an inherited, progressive genetic disease that primarily affects the lungs and digestive system. A defect in the CFTR gene leads to thick, sticky mucus that clogs the airways and pancreas. This is a plain-language summary — link to the CFF "Intro to CF" page (`https://www.cff.org/intro-cf`) rather than restating clinical detail here.

## History

The narrative below follows one arc — **discovery → survival → modulators → today** — and every dated claim is tied to the Foundation's primary history page. Where we paraphrase, the [source](https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history) is cited rather than the clinical detail restated.

### Discovery

- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **1938** — Dorothy Andersen, MD, published the first characterization of the disease.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **1955** — A group of concerned parents, determined to save the lives of their children, founded the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **1989** — The CFTR gene and protein were discovered by Foundation-supported scientists. (The 1989 discovery is the hinge the whole modulator era hangs on; we link rather than explain the channel biology.)

### Survival trajectory

- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] In **1955**, the Foundation says, children born with CF were not expected to live long enough to attend elementary school.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **Today**, the Foundation says people with CF are living into their 30s, 40s and beyond. (This is the single clearest statement of progress in the source; it is the Foundation's claim, and we do not extrapolate a life-expectancy number the source does not give.)
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2022** — The CF population was estimated at roughly **40,000 in the U.S.** and **105,000 worldwide**.

> The jump from "not expected to attend elementary school" to "living into their 30s, 40s, and beyond" is the story the community tells first. We treat it as the Foundation's own framing, not as a lab claim.

### Modulators and drug development

A note on scope: we list **what the Foundation's history page states** about each approval (year, brand, and the one-line significance it gives). We do not build a clinical indication table — per the `cf-research-context` skill, drug/approval detail links out to the CFF and primary regulatory sources.

- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **1993** — FDA approves **Pulmozyme®**, the first drug developed specifically for CF.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **1997** — FDA approves **TOBI®**, the first aerosolized antibiotic for CF; the Therapeutics Development Program is established.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2010** — FDA approves **Cayston®**.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2012** — FDA approves **ivacaftor (Kalydeco®)**, described by the Foundation as the first treatment to address the underlying cause.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2015** — FDA approves **Orkambi®** (lumacaftor/ivacaftor).
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2018** — FDA approves **Symdeko®** (tezacaftor/ivacaftor).
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2019** — FDA approves **Trikafta®** (elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor), the first triple-combination modulator.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2024** — FDA approves **Alyftrek®** (vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor), the first once-a-day modulator; the source also notes **Trikafta** expanded to additional rare mutations.

### The Foundation's financial model in the timeline

These are the funding/venture events the history page records — included because they explain *how* the drugs above got funded, not as a list of products.

- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2000** — $40M investment in Aurora Biosciences (later Vertex), the first test of the venture-philanthropy model.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2014** — The Foundation sells royalty rights to Vertex for **$3.3 billion**.
- [verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/our-history] **2019** — The **Path to a Cure** portfolio reaches $500M.

### Boundary

This is a research reference, not medical advice. Clinical detail, current indications, and dosing belong at the [CFF](https://www.cff.org) and primary regulatory sources, not in these repos. We never round, restate, or extend a clinical claim beyond what a cited source says.

## The 65 roses

[verified, 2026-10-03, https://www.cff.org/about-us/65-roses-story] "65 Roses" began with a child's pronunciation of cystic fibrosis in 1965. The Foundation describes the rose as a symbol of its work; the story is not about a founding patient count. Source: [CFF — 65 Roses story](https://www.cff.org/about-us/65-roses-story). Our exactly **65** rose motifs are an independent tribute, not an official emblem or affiliation.

## What we support / how we engage

- Research is shared **open source** and built in public — consistent with the Foundation's "Community" pillar.
- This lab does not collect or forward donations. Giving to the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation, through its Washington chapter or nationally, is done with the Foundation directly.
- No patient data (PHI) belongs in these repos. Follow the control repo's `security-baseline` and `cf-research-context` skills before handling medical research material.

## Structures of CFTR

Four published structures of human CFTR (6MSM, 5UAK, 8EIQ and 8EJ1) are compared in `landscape/cftr-structures.md`, with the numbers, the limits and what the files declare. `tools/cftr-model/` draws them in 3D from the same files. Neither says how any medicine acts or whether a channel is open; they show where the residues the files place sit.
